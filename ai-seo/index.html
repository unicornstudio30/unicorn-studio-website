What we ship

Five layers of AI-search optimisation.

AI SEO is not a bolt-on. It's a systematic overhaul: technical foundation, content structure, machine-readable signals, monitoring, and iteration. We do all five so citations start showing up and keep showing up.

01Foundation

Technical AI-readiness audit

Every AI crawler (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot, and 15 more) has different requirements. We audit your robots.txt, llms.txt, sitemap, canonicals, structured data, and Markdown twins against every scanner spec in production today.

  • AI-readiness scorecard (baseline + target)
  • llms.txt, robots.txt, sitemap.xml optimised for AI crawlers
  • Well-known endpoints (api-catalog, OAuth, signatures) where relevant
02Structure

Schema.org + JSON-LD everywhere

Organization, WebSite, Article, FAQPage, HowTo, Product, Service, BreadcrumbList, Person. Every page gets the schema types that match its content, so Google's AI Overview and LLM search can pull structured answers directly.

  • JSON-LD blocks on every route
  • Rich-result-eligible schema (Google Search Console validated)
  • Author + Organization graph for E-E-A-T signals
03Content

LLM-optimised content rewrites

AI search rewards direct answers, specific numbers, cited claims, and tight semantic clustering. We rewrite your key pages so the answer to a query is in the first 200 words, backed by data your competitors don't cite.

  • Answer-first paragraphs on every commercial page
  • Specific numbers + dates + named entities baked in
  • Markdown twin (/path/index.md) for every page
04Distribution

AI citation seeding

LLM training data comes from specific sources: Common Crawl, Wikipedia, high-authority blogs, forums, GitHub, YouTube transcripts. We help you get named in those sources so future model rounds bake you in.

  • High-authority guest posts and citations
  • Wikipedia + Wikidata presence where warranted
  • Structured PR and thought-leadership publishing plan
05Reporting

AI citation monitoring

Weekly report on where your brand shows up in AI answers vs traditional search. Track queries where you appear in Google AI Overview, get cited in Perplexity, or come up in ChatGPT search. We rewrite whatever isn't converting.

  • Weekly AI-citation dashboard
  • Query-level tracking across 4-5 AI engines
  • Bi-weekly optimisation call to steer next iteration

AI SEO

Common questions about AI SEO.

The ones founders and operators ask before we sign. Don’t see yours?

Ask on a discovery call

Traditional SEO optimises for Google's blue links: keywords, backlinks, page speed. AI SEO optimises for machine consumption: structured data, direct-answer paragraphs, machine-readable content surfaces (Markdown twins, llms.txt), and getting cited by name in AI training data. We do both because AI engines still pull from the traditional index. But the tactics that move the needle are different.

Google AI Overview (biggest volume), Perplexity, ChatGPT search, Claude web search, Gemini, DuckAssist, You.com, and Bing Copilot. Each has slightly different signals; we optimise for the underlying patterns rather than gaming any single engine.

For Google AI Overview and Perplexity, yes — those are traceable and we back them with a work-for-free guarantee. For ChatGPT's default answers, no honest agency can guarantee that; the training cutoffs and grounding are opaque. What we can guarantee is that when ChatGPT search does browse your site, it will find well-structured, machine-readable content.

Maybe. But so is every SEO signal until enough engines honor it. Right now llms.txt costs us nothing to ship, is honored by Perplexity and Anthropic's crawlers, and doesn't break anything. Same logic applies to Markdown twins, api-catalog, and the well-known endpoints. Cheap insurance against tomorrow's convention.

Content matters, but structure matters more for AI. A perfectly-written article without JSON-LD, canonicals, and machine-readable twins won't get cited by an AI agent. And a mediocre article with all the right signals often will. We fix the signal layer first, then the content layer.
